 The first British and the second man to reach the South Pole. He and other two crew member died on the returned journey due to the loss of direction and exacerbating health condition, only 11 miles away from a resupply depot.[1](#ref-1) One of the misconceptions corrected by Scott in his 1904 expedition was that Antarctic was assumed to be closely bounded in ice, but it is possible to made a traverse in February without encountering pack-ice.[2](#ref-2) There was a misconception that the glacier’s recession is cause by progressive moderation of the climate or the incoming warm period – Scott found no evidence to support that; instead, the evidence he collected proved that the recession is cause by coldness where not enough precipitation was created to sustain the growth of the glacier in Antarctic.[3](#ref-3) In 1907, Scott brought a captive balloon to Antarctic, while Shackleton brought a motor vehicle to the continent not long after.[4](#ref-4)

 One [American](/dictionary/asst) and one [New Zealand](/dictionary/sst) Antarctic Station are named after him.
