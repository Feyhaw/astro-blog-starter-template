A scientific concept referring to living beings on Earth as a geological force, or a title of book written by Russian-Ukrainian scientist [Vladimir I. Vernadsky](/dictionary/vivernadsky) in 1926. Although the word “biosphere” wasn’t coined by him, Vladimir I. Vernadsky contributed to the concept by recognizing its role as an active shaping force of the Earth surface.[1](#ref-1) Vernadsky also used the words “Anthropocene” or “noosphere” to denote the human’s importance in the geological process.[2](#ref-2) Vernadsky’s 1926 book Biosphere did not have an English translation until 1985.[3](#ref-3) Following Vernadsky, Mikhail Mikhailovich Kamshilov recognized that biomes are the building blocks of Biosphere; they are large assemblages of plants and animals, characterized by dominant vegetation and climate.[4](#ref-4) Later [Alexander Chizhevsky](/dictionary/alxchizhevsky) also familiarized himself with this theory and developed his solar cycle hypothesis.[4](#ref-4)
