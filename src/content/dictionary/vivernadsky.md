Vernadsky was born to an intellectual family and he stood for intellectual freedom and integrity against all contrary pressures, whether from Tsarist or Soviet.[1](#ref-1) He privately and openly criticize classic Marxist and Soviet tendencies of affecting natural science.[2](#ref-2) In Lysenko affair which led to disastrous famine and crippling of life science in USSR for decades, Vernadsky spoke out to liberate scientific endeavours from political, cultural, and religious constraints.[3](#ref-3) He was arrested briefly but was then spared from harsh punishment to allow him continuing research across many fields, many of which were demanded by the military; his patriotism is unquestionable, especially when he refused to flee to the United States with his son.[4](#ref-4)

As a scientist, he was chiefly known for his concept of Earth surface evolving from biosphere into noosphere.[5](#ref-5) In contrary to population views, he did not firstly coined the words “biosphere” and “noosphere,” but elaborated these ideas in a body of scientific and philosophical speculations.[6](#ref-6) He demonstrated how living beings transfer solar energy into mineral matter as an exchange with nature; thus, humans are connected to the planet and other living beings through physical and chemical interactions.[7](#ref-7) He established long-term exchange with religious sector, which he thought was not conflicting with science, as everything on earth is connected.[8](#ref-8) He presented the “migration of atoms and their particles within living matter and between living and inert matter.”[9](#ref-9) 

He noted “Radiations from all stars enter the biosphere, but we and perceive only an insignificant part of the total: this comes almost exclusively from the sun,”[10](#ref-10) this had laid the foundation for [Chizhevsky’s](/dictionary/alxchizhevsky) thesis. Vernadsky believed in the eventual depletion of resources in biosphere, and humans would have to make some radical changes or perish.[11](#ref-11) Currently, one [Ukraine Anatrctic station](/dictionary/vernadskyst) was named after him.
