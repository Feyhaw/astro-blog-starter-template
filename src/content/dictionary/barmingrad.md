Also known as [Zvezda](/dictionary/zvezda). Named after Vladimir Barmin by his colleague Valentin Glushko, who were the Soviet’s top rocket engineer, and later would become a powerful figure in Soviet space program.[1](#ref-1) His lab design the rocket engine RD 107 and RD 108.[2] It is a Soviet space project initiated in 1962, aiming to build a base on the surface of the moon and colonize it for industrial use.[3](#ref-3) 

According to Paul Meuser, it was the world’s first detailed design for the city outside the Earth. The design comprises 9 cylindrical blocks which would be manufactured on Earth. Blocks have the size of 8.6 meters in length and a 3.3-meter diameter, and will host one of the functions of: three bedrooms, a command post, a science laboratory, a warehouse, a workshop, a first-aid post, a fitness room, a kitchen with dinning space. The size was compressible, and pressured air must be injected so the modules can reach the full length. It was approved by the Soviet defence minister, but after a series of failure of the heavy rocket N-1, the project was scrapped in 1974.[4](#ref-4)

<strong>A glimpse of the background</strong>

It is worth noting that, comparing to the United States, Soviet Union had ambitious plans for long-term stay in space since the beginning of the Space Age. To figure out how spaceflight may influence man and woman's bodily function, [Vostok-5](/dictionary/vvtereshkova) and Vostok-6 were launched in a joint mission.[5](#ref-5) Since Soviet Union prioritized interplanetary flight over lunar flight (opposite to the US), a sophisticated study for bioregenerative life sustaining system was developed.[6](#ref-6)
