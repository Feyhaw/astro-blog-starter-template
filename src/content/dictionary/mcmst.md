Initially built in 1956 as a Naval base of the United States for America in preparation for the [International Geophysical Year](/dictionary/igy). Named after the nearby McMurdo Sound, it was originally called McMurdo Naval Air Field, or McMurdo NAF, McMurdo Station gained permanent status in 1961 due to the difficulty to continually maintain other stations in 1961.[1](#ref-1) 

In the same year, a small nuclear reactor, PM-3 was built at McMurdo Station on the Observer Hill to supply the electricity. It became operational in July 1962.[2] The powerplant was initially built and operated by Martin company, [Atomic Energy Commission](/dictionary/aec) personnels, and military representatives. The powerplant was shut down in 1972 when a leak was detected, and due to the high cost to full inspect the plant, decisions were made to permanently shut down the reactor.[3](#ref-3) Defueling and decommissioning began in the Arctic summer of October 1973.

McMurdo Station was reached by the first commercial flight to Antarctic on October 15, 1957 by Pan Am.[4](#ref-4) Like Little America V, McMurdo Station was initially created for the supply of other Antarctic research stations during the 1955 Operation Deep Freeze. The US Navy (Department of Defence) began to handover the station to National Science Foundation in 1970, and in 1972, the station was under the direct lead of NSF. The long transitioning process is only finished the process was finished as late as 1998, although the supply of the station was still provided by military aircrafts.[5](#ref-5)
