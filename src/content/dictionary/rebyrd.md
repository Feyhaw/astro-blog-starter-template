 American explorer and Naval officer, the first man to fly over South Pole in 1929.[1](#ref-1) During this expedition he also established the first American station Little America I. It was this time the Antarctic was connected to the rest of the world through radio, by the effort of one of his crew [Lloyd Berkner](/dictionary/lloydberkner).[2](#ref-2) 
 
 <strong>Antarctic negotiation</strong>

 On November 25, 1939, President Roosevelt wrote a letter to Byrd, who was at the time the commander of the (later to be) short-lived United States Antarctic Service, that cautioned the members not to make any statements that could compromise the American government’s traditional policy, which does not recognize and territorial claims in Antarctic.[3](#ref-3) In 1946, he organized Operation High Jump in 1946, which took aerial photos of Antarctica.[4](#ref-4) In 1955 he was appointed as the top policy officer of the Defense Establish on the Arctic matters.[5](#ref-5) He and Edward Shackleton (son of [Ernest Shackleton](/dictionary/ehshackleton)) agreed on New Zealand Prime Minister suggested in January of 1956 to entrust Antarctic under the UN control, but the proposal was reject by Australian government.[6](#ref-6) After he deceased in 1957, which marked the ending of the “heroic age.” his successor, Rear Admiral George Dufek, continuef leading Navy to support post IGY activities.[7](#ref-7) 
 
 One of American stations was named after him; it is one of the three stations American controlled by 1962.
