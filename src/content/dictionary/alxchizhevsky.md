<strong>Tsiolkovsky's student and friend</strong>

Also translated as “Chizhevskii” or “Tchijevsky.” He was born to an elite family and privately educated.[1](#ref-1) Chizhevsky’s mother died when he was little and his father devoted all of his energy to Chizhevsky’s education. He was brought to France and Italy, and studied in a Paris art school; then he served in father’s regiment.[2](#ref-2) In 1913, Chizhevsky moved to Kaluga due his father’s military mobilization, there he met [Konstantin Tsiolkovsky](/dictionary/tsiolkovsky) who was serving as a village teacher.[3](#ref-3) The two became life-long friends. In later years, Tsiolkovsky provided public support to Chizhevsky when his dissertations were attacked; and in return, Chizhevsky wrote the preface for Tsiolkovsky’s book on rocketry after he had established himself in the Soviet Union.[4](#ref-4)

<strong>Development of heliobiology</strong>

In 1918, he started develop his theory on heliobilogy, which studies the relationship between solar cycle and historical process. The original title is “Investigation of the Relationship between the Sunspot Activity and the Course of the Universal Historical Process from the V Century to the Present Day.”[5](#ref-5) This formed his foundation of his PhD dissertation. He tried to combine the known observation of solar perturbation and cycles of major historical events of human societies, such as wars and revolutions. He was inspired by the new concept of biosphere where the effect of solar energy can be translated to human society through matter and energy.[6](#ref-6)

He observed that the solar cycles have an average length of 12 years, and can be divided into four periods: minimum excitability (3 yrs), growth excitability (2 yrs), maximum excitability (3 yrs), and decline excitability (3 yrs).[7](#ref-7) The first period is characterized by general peace and tolerance to the different belief despite the lack of unity, there is the absence of desire and idea of struggle; the second period is defined by the increase of political unity, ideas, and leadership, and built up of military tension; The third period is characterized by the maximum level of excitement, greatest insanity and achievements of states, biggest wars and revolutions, massive integration of individuals, and is the most eventful of all; The last period is characterized by the receding enthusiasm and embrace for peace, mass movement dies out.[8](#ref-8)

<strong>Political prosecution under Stalin</strong>

Although Chizhevsky acknowledged that humans have free-will despite solar flutuation, such theory was nevertheless criticized by the communist regime as non-Marxist (history is only crerated by "people").[9](#ref-9) In 1942, He was arrested and sentenced sixteen years in prison and exile, from 1942 to 1958.[10](#ref-10) He managed to – though not voluntarily – terminate a cholera outbreak in prison with bleaching powder and other makeshift remedies.[11](#ref-11) He was then awarded with a chance to set up a laboratory in prison clinic, where he conducted “groundbreaking” research on blood movements and won recognition from authority after his rehabilitation.[12](#ref-12) He returned to Moscow in 1958 and passed away in 1962.
