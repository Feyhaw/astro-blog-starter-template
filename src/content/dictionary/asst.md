American South Pole station. Built in 1956 during the [International Geophysical Year](/dictionary/igy). It was named after the Norwegian expeditioner [Roald Amundsen](/dictionary/ramundsen) and [Rober F. Scott](/dictionary/rfscott), who reach the South Pole on December 11, 1911 and January 18, 1912 respectively. 

The station was originally called the "South Pole station." It was one of the 6 stations built for the IGY during the 1955 Operation Deep Freeze.[1](#ref-1) While the other stations became inaccessible due to snow issue, Amundsen-Scott remianed to be one of the three American stations (the other two are Byrd Station and McMurdo Station).[2] In 1970s, a geodesic dome of TEMCOR company ([Buckminster Fuller](/dictionary/bucky) was a Board member) was built as the new station. The current structure was built in 2000.
