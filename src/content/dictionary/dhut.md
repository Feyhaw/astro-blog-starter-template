A wooden single-floor structure located 300 meters south from McMurdo Station. It was built by [Robert Scott](/dictionary/rfscott) and his crew during the 1904 expedition. The main accommodation of Scott’s 1901-1904 Discovery expedition is the ship Discovery, the hut built in early 1902 was used for auxiliary use such as storage, workshop, theatre, magnetic observation, and emergency shelter.[1](#ref-1) 

<strong>Features</strong>

The discovery hut was square on footprint with the sides of 10.3m and 1.5m wide verandahs on three sides used for storage. The base sunk 1.2m into permafrost, the floor boards are above and below joists. The hut was designed with three sections: laboratory, crew quarter, and officer quarter. Despite the size, there is no ventilation system.[2](#ref-2) Only one of the two stoves were installed thus caused the temperature to be unbearable if no extra measures were implemented. Lighting was provided by candles and blubber lamps.

The hut was designed by Professor J. W. Gregory, who lived in London and had experience in polar expedition, but only in tents.  He was appointed as the Chair of Geology of the University of Melbourne. The hut was prefabricated in Sydney for 360 pounds and assembled in Melbourne.[3](#ref-3) Scott described the hut as an Australian bungalow, typical in Queensland where a pyramidal roof covers verandah, providing cooling and shading.  The expedition carpenter found it difficult to assemble the hut again in Antarctic due to the warping of the timber as the result of its exposure to sea water, which turned out to be a common issue for the following huts. It has too many windows and the inside is poorly heated, it demonstrates the poor adaptability of the original Australian design, if left unchanged.

<strong>Later use</strong>

It was used by Shackleton in three succeeding expeditions in 1908, 1911, and 1915. In 1914, [Shackleton](/dictionary/ehshackleton) used it to deposit supplies,[4](#ref-4) and in 1915, Shackleton and his crewed took refugee in Discovery hut for four months. In 1956, American crews [settled camps](/dictionary/mcmst) around the hut,[5](#ref-5) making it the only architecture that served four separate expeditions. The layout of the hut was modified every time to suit particular need. In 1964 and 1965, the hut was restored by New Zealand authorities, and is now a protected relic.[6](#ref-6) Interestingly, when it was built, it was regarded the least successful Antarctic hut to that date.
